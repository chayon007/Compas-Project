   - Random perturbations don't fool the model
   - Code-mixing similarly has minimal effect

PAPER IMPACT (Axis C):
This demonstrates that while Bangla hate speech models perform well on standard
benchmarks, they suffer critical fragility when exposed to transliteration attacks.
Roman script variants (used frequently on social media) can bypass detection,
allowing hateful content in transliterated form.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
- Social media platforms must augment training data with Romanized variants
- Transliteration normalization preprocessing is essential
- Current models are NOT production-ready for multilingual input

   - Model disproportionately flags mixed dialect as hate (over-censorship)

🔴 EQUALIZED ODDS FAILURE: Gap of 0.2089 far exceeds fairness threshold (<0.1)
   - Violates fairness requirement for equalized opportunity
   - Affects treatment parity in content moderation

⚠️ SIGNIFICANT F1 GAP: 7.35% difference between dialect groups
   - Standard dialect performs well (F1 = 0.755)
   - Mixed dialect significantly underperforms (F1 = 0.682)
   - Indicates algorithmic bias in feature representation

PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS:
The model exhibits ALGORITHMIC BIAS against mixed/non-standard dialects:
• Risk of disproportionate content removal for speakers using mixed dialect
• Potential silencing of marginalized linguistic communities
• Violates fairness principles required for responsible AI deployment

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
- Content moderation systems using this model will discriminate
- Mixed dialect speakers disproportionately affected
- Violates fairness requirements in AI governance
- Needs fairness-aware training before production deployment

PAPER IMPACT (Axis B):
